Data and method
  • UX1 is the nearest monthly VX future that has not yet expired on that date; UX7 is the seventh. Weekly VX contracts are excluded. Daily settlement prices, falling back to the close where Cboe's older files report a zero settle.
  • Sources: Cboe VX futures historical files (2013 on) and the Cboe CFE archive (2008–2012); ES and VIX closes from Yahoo Finance. ES is Yahoo's continuous front-month series (ES=F). It is not back-adjusted, so each quarterly roll leaves a small step of a few points. Yahoo has no ES close for 2016-10-10 and 2016-11-11; the line bridges those days.
  • Coverage runs 2008-01-02 to 2026-10-09. Days when futures traded but the stock market was closed are dropped.
  • From 2008-02-20 to 2008-04-18 Cboe had not yet listed the September 2008 contract, so UX7 on those 42 days is the seventh listed month (November 2008), as in a generic UX7 series.
  • Dislocations follow the paper's Eq. 4: each of UX1–UX7 is ranked by price (one plus the number of the seven that are strictly cheaper), and the count is how many contracts don't sit at their own rank. Perfect contango scores 0 and a fully inverted curve scores 6, because UX4 stays in the middle. Bars are colored by the paper's regimes: low (up to 4) and high (5–7). Perfect-contango days have no bar. On 95 days two neighboring contracts settled at the same price, which Eq. 4 counts as a single dislocation, so a count of 1 appears on those days.
